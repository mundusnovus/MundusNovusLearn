# --description--

You have reached the last module. Its project is the real test of everything you have learned: an app of **your own**, designed by you, shipped to real people.

## Choose well

The best first app solves a small problem you actually have. You will use it every day, so you will notice what is wrong with it, and you will finish it because you want it to exist. Some shapes that work well:

- a tool for a hobby (a climbing log, a sourdough timer, a chord finder),
- something for your community (a church rota, a club's fixtures, a food bank's opening hours),
- a calm, focused version of an app you find too noisy.

Keep the first version **small**: one main screen, one core action, done beautifully. You can always add more in version 1.1.

## Write it down

Before opening Xcode, write a one-page plan:

1. **The problem**, in one sentence.
2. **Who it is for.**
3. **User stories**: five to ten "I can…" sentences, like the projects in this course. Mark the three that matter most.
4. **The data**: your models and their properties.
5. **The screens**: a rough sketch of each, on paper.
6. **Platforms**: iPhone first? Mac too? A widget?

# --questions--

## What makes a good first app?
- [x] A small app that solves a problem you have yourself
- [ ] The biggest app you can imagine
- [ ] A copy of a popular app
Why: You will use it, notice what is wrong, and finish it.

# --description--

Build the app you planned, and ship it.

There is no template and no test suite. What you have instead is everything in this course, Apple's documentation, the Swift community, and your own judgement, which has grown a great deal since your first `print`.

Work in small steps. Get the most important user story working end to end first, even if it is ugly. Then the next. Commit to Git often. Put the app on your phone early, and use it.

When it does its core job well, send it to a few friends through TestFlight. Listen more than you explain. Fix what confuses them. Then submit it to the App Store.

When it is live, you are an app developer. Congratulations, and well done.

> **Tip** Share what you made on **Asteria Social**, and tell us what this course could do better. It is open source; you can improve it yourself, at [github.com/mundusnovus/MundusNovusLearn](https://github.com/mundusnovus/MundusNovusLearn).

# --checklist--

- My app's three most important user stories work.
- It has tests for its core logic, and passes an accessibility audit.
- At least three people tested it through TestFlight, and I acted on their feedback.
- It has a privacy-respecting design, and accurate privacy labels.
- I submitted it to the App Store.

## Your testers find your app confusing. What is the best response?
- [ ] Add a long tutorial explaining it
- [x] Watch how they use it, and change the design so it explains itself
- [ ] Ignore them: they will learn
Why: The best interface needs no instructions.
